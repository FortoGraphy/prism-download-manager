Comparison

The best free download managers in 2026

Twelve download managers for Windows, macOS and Linux, compared on what you can check yourself: price, source code, platforms, browser integration, torrents, video and how actively they’re updated — including the catches.

By Jan Sechovec, the developer of Prism · Updated 27 November 2026

Full disclosure: this page is written by the developer of Prism, one of the apps below. To keep it fair, every fact about the other apps comes from their official sites, release pages and source code, checked on 27 November 2026 — sources are at the end. Prism’s own weak spots are listed too.

Quick picks

  • Free all-rounder for Windows, Mac and Linux: Prism — open source, browser extension, video, torrents and Hugging Face models in one app. Young project, though.
  • You want the classic and don’t mind paying: Internet Download Manager — Windows only, US$24.95 for a lifetime licence.
  • File hosts and link lists: JDownloader 2 — unmatched plugins for hosting sites.
  • Simple and polished, no torrents needed: AB Download Manager — open source, on desktop and Android.
  • Phones too: Gopeed — Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iOS.
  • Command line and scripts: aria2.
  • Mostly torrents: a dedicated BitTorrent client such as qBittorrent.

Each app: good for, watch out for

Prism

Good for: one free, open-source app on all three desktop systems that covers ordinary downloads (up to 32 connections per file, resume after crashes and power cuts), browser capture, video and music from YouTube and 1,800+ sites, torrents and magnet links, and AI models from Hugging Face checked against their SHA-256. Built in Rust; light on memory; no ads, no account, no telemetry.

Watch out for: it’s a young project (first release in October 2026). The installers aren’t code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen asks once. There’s no phone app, no plugins for file-hosting sites like JDownloader’s, and its torrent support is basic: no incoming connections or port settings yet.

Internet Download Manager (IDM)

Good for: the benchmark everyone compares against. Mature, polished browser integration and video grabbing on Windows, with up to 32 connections.

Watch out for: it’s paid after a 30-day trial (US$24.95 for a lifetime licence for one PC), closed source and Windows only — no Mac or Linux version, and no torrents.

Free Download Manager (FDM)

Good for: a free, ad-free app on Windows, Mac, Linux and Android, with torrents built in.

Watch out for: it hasn’t been open source since version 3.9.7; current versions are closed freeware. Video download needs a separate add-on. Between 2020 and 2022, the official site served a backdoored Linux package to some visitors, which came to light in 2023 — something to know if you install it on Linux.

AB Download Manager

Good for: a polished, modern-looking open-source manager with queues, schedulers and several themes, on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android. Actively developed since 2024, and one of the most-starred download managers on GitHub.

Watch out for: no torrents or magnet links (a long-standing feature request), and its developer has declined YouTube support; it can download HLS video streams.

JDownloader 2

Good for: file-hosting sites and long link lists. Its plugins handle captchas, wait times and folder links on hundreds of hosts better than anything else here.

Watch out for: the main Windows installer can include optional third-party offers — pick the installer without them on jdownloader.org. In May 2026, attackers briefly replaced some installers on the official site with malware; the in-app updates weren’t affected. It runs on Java and its interface is busy. No torrents.

Xtreme Download Manager (XDM)

Good for: an open-source IDM-style manager for all three systems, with wide browser support. Version 9, a rewrite, arrived in October 2026.

Watch out for: YouTube is deliberately blocked, and before version 9 the project went years without a stable release.

Motrix

Good for: a clean, open-source front end for the aria2 engine, with torrents and up to 64 connections per server. Version 2, a full rewrite, shipped in October 2026.

Watch out for: no video downloads from sites, and its Windows packages are unsigned.

Gopeed

Good for: the widest platform list: Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS and a web version. Torrents, magnet and ed2k links, and an extension system.

Watch out for: YouTube and many site features come from extensions you install separately. Version 2.0 is brand new (November 2026).

Ghost Downloader

Good for: an actively developed open-source manager with torrents (libtorrent) and video through yt-dlp, on desktop and Android.

Watch out for: the desktop app needs a CPU with AVX support, and the developer has said updates will be less frequent for a while.

Mister Download Manager

Good for: an open-source manager that deliberately copies IDM’s interface, with torrents and video through yt-dlp.

Watch out for: a very young project (started at the end of October 2026), Windows and Linux only for now with macOS marked experimental, no code signing yet, and machine-translated interface languages. Its speed comparisons with IDM are its own tests.

Neat Download Manager

Good for: a small, simple free manager for Windows and Mac with a browser extension.

Watch out for: closed source and apparently unmaintained — the current versions date from 2022 (Windows) and 2021 (Mac).

aria2

Good for: scripts, servers and power users. A tiny command-line downloader for HTTP, FTP, BitTorrent and Metalink, and the engine inside Motrix and others.

Watch out for: no graphical interface or browser integration of its own, and no release since November 2023.

How to choose

  • Windows 11 or 10, free: Prism, AB Download Manager, XDM, Ghost Downloader, Motrix or FDM. If you want IDM’s exact workflow, Prism and Mister Download Manager are the closest free ones — see Prism as an IDM alternative.
  • Mac: IDM doesn’t run on macOS, and Mister only experimentally. Prism, AB Download Manager, FDM, Motrix, Gopeed, XDM, Ghost Downloader, JDownloader and Neat do — see Prism for Mac.
  • Linux: Prism, AB Download Manager, XDM, Motrix, Gopeed, Ghost Downloader, JDownloader and aria2 — see Prism for Linux.
  • You only care about open source: skip IDM, FDM and Neat.
  • You download AI models: Prism downloads Hugging Face models by name, picks only the files you need and verifies them.

Are download managers really faster?

Only where the server is the bottleneck. Many servers limit the speed of each connection; a download manager opens several connections and adds them up. In our own tests on such a server, a 1 GiB file came down about 15× faster than over a single connection. When the server doesn’t limit anything, your internet line is the limit for every app on this page — any “10× faster than IDM” claim is measured on a test setup, not your connection. We haven’t benchmarked the other GUI apps here; our benchmarks compare Prism with curl and aria2. More in how to download faster on a PC.

FAQ

Choosing a download manager, answered.

What is the best free download manager?

It depends on what you download. For an all-in-one free and open-source app on Windows, Mac and Linux with a browser extension, video and torrents, Prism is a strong choice (we make it, so judge for yourself). JDownloader 2 is best for file-hosting sites, Gopeed if you also want phone apps, and aria2 for the command line.

What is the best download manager for Windows 11?

IDM is the best-known paid option. Free ones that work well on Windows 11 include Prism, AB Download Manager, XDM, Motrix, Ghost Downloader, FDM and JDownloader 2. If you want IDM’s workflow without paying, try an open-source IDM alternative such as Prism.

Is there a free version of IDM?

No. IDM has a 30-day trial and then needs a licence (US$24.95 for one PC, lifetime). Free and open-source alternatives with similar features include Prism, AB Download Manager, XDM and Mister Download Manager.

What is the best download manager for Mac?

IDM doesn’t run on macOS. Free options include Prism, AB Download Manager, FDM, Motrix, Gopeed, XDM and Ghost Downloader; Prism runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.

Are free download managers safe?

Reputable ones are, if you download them from the official site or GitHub and not from third-party download portals, which sometimes bundle adware. Open-source apps let anyone inspect the code; check that the project is still updated.
